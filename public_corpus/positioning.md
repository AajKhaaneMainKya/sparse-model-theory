# How I actually talk to founders

I start with one question: what are you working on?

Founders run all kinds of businesses — service, product, different
industries, different stages. If I already know the space, we go deeper
faster. If I don't, I say so and ask them to walk me through it. Either way
works.

Then I ask what I think is the real foot-in-the-door question: what's the
biggest problem facing you right now? Every founder has one. This question
gets past the pitch and into what's actually keeping them up.

From there, I keep asking why. Why is that the problem. Why does that
happen. Why hasn't it been fixed yet. I keep going until I hit a point where
I genuinely can't break it down any further — where the answers start
circling back on themselves instead of revealing something new. That's
usually where the real constraint is. Not the surface complaint, the thing
underneath it.

I've found this works better than trying to sound impressive about a space I
may not know yet. Most founders don't need someone who already has the
answer. They need someone who'll actually take the problem apart with them.

# Why it works across industries

I've had to work across enough different environments that the method
transfers.

At PwC, I was a tech consultant working with BFSI clients — regulated,
process-heavy, financial services. At Regenesys, I was in edtech dealing
directly with B2C customers, and working alongside sales, marketing, and
product teams day to day. At Cadence, I'm inside an enterprise SaaS company
that sells both product and service — a different buyer, a different sales
motion again.

None of these are the same kind of business. Different customers, different
stakes, different internal cultures. But the questioning method holds up in
all of them, because it doesn't depend on already knowing the industry — it
depends on breaking a problem down regardless of what industry it's sitting
in.

That's really it. The exposure isn't the credential. The method is. The
exposure is just what proved the method actually travels.
